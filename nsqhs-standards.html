Safety and quality · Study guide

The NSQHS Standards, one card each

The National Safety and Quality Health Service (NSQHS) Standards are developed by the Australian Commission on Safety and Quality in Health Care (ACSQHC). Hospitals and day procedure services across Australia are accredited against them.

You will hear them mentioned on placement, in audits and in assignments. Here is what each one is about and what it looks like at the bedside.

Quick facts

  • The current version is the second edition, which has 8 standards.
  • Each standard includes a description, a statement of intent, criteria and the actions a health service must meet.
  • The ACSQHC is developing a third edition. It is still in draft, and services keep using the second edition in the meantime. Check the ACSQHC website for updates.
Standard 1

Clinical Governance

Sets up the leadership, culture and systems that keep care safe and high quality, and keep improving it.

On placement you might see

  • Orientation, scope of practice and working under supervision.
  • Reporting incidents and near misses so the system can learn.
  • Following evidence-based local guidance.
  • A safe physical environment for patients and staff.

Student tip: If something feels unsafe, you have a role in raising it. Know how to report incidents where you are placed.

Standard 2

Partnering with Consumers

Puts people at the centre of care, as partners in decisions about their own care and in how health services are designed.

On placement you might see

  • Informed consent and shared decision-making.
  • Bedside handover that includes the patient.
  • Checking understanding with teach-back.
  • Using interpreters and information people can understand.

Student tip: Ask the patient what matters to them, and include them when you hand over.

Standard 3

Preventing and Controlling Infections

Prevents infections, manages them well when they happen, and supports careful use of antimicrobials.

On placement you might see

  • Hand hygiene at the 5 moments.
  • Standard and transmission-based precautions, including PPE.
  • Aseptic technique (for example ANTT).
  • Antimicrobial stewardship, cleaning, and staff vaccination.

Student tip: Hand hygiene is the habit everyone notices. Get it right every time.

Standard 4

Medication Safety

Makes sure medicines are prescribed, dispensed, given and monitored safely, and that patients understand their medicines.

On placement you might see

  • Taking a best possible medication history and reconciling medicines.
  • Documenting allergies and adverse drug reactions.
  • Checking processes before giving any medicine.
  • Giving patients clear information about their medicines.

Student tip: Never give a medicine you do not understand. Look it up and ask your supervisor first.

Standard 5

Comprehensive Care

Delivers coordinated care based on a thorough assessment and a care plan developed with the patient, with a focus on minimising harm.

On placement you might see

  • Screening and risk assessment on admission and when things change.
  • Preventing pressure injuries and falls.
  • Nutrition and hydration, and recognising delirium and cognitive impairment.
  • Minimising restrictive practices, plus end-of-life care.

Student tip: Risk assessments are only useful if the care plan acts on them. Link what you find to what you do.

Standard 6

Communicating for Safety

Makes sure critical information is communicated clearly and on time between clinicians, patients and families.

On placement you might see

  • Correct patient identification at every handover, transfer and procedure.
  • Structured clinical handover, such as ISBAR.
  • Communicating critical information and results quickly.
  • Clear, accurate and timely documentation.

Student tip: Use ISBAR when you hand over or escalate. Structure helps others hear the key points.

Standard 7

Blood Management

Supports good management of a patient’s own blood and the safe, appropriate use of blood and blood products.

On placement you might see

  • Patient blood management, including recognising and treating anaemia.
  • Consent for transfusion.
  • Correct patient identification and checking before transfusion.
  • Monitoring for and responding to transfusion reactions.

Student tip: Know the observations required before, during and after a transfusion where you are placed.

Standard 8

Recognising and Responding to Acute Deterioration

Makes sure deterioration in a patient’s physical or mental state is recognised early and acted on.

On placement you might see

  • Accurate, regular observations recorded on observation charts.
  • Escalating care when observations or your concern change.
  • Rapid response systems.
  • Patients, families and carers can raise concerns and escalate care.

Student tip: Worried is a valid reason to escalate. Trust the trend and your instincts.
