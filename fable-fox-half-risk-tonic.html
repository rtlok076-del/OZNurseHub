The Fox’s Half-Risk Tonic

A small story about a tonic that promised to cut the risk in half, and why “half” means little until you ask: half of what?

Watch · 1 minuteRead · 2 minutesLearning & reflection

The full story

Half the risk sounds huge. But half of what?

One morning at the lantern market, the mocking fox set up a shiny stall covered in tiny glass bottles. “Marsh-Fever Tonic!” he cried. “Cuts your risk of marsh fever in HALF! Fifty per cent safer!”

A crowd of rabbits, ducks and hedgehogs pushed forward, coins in their paws. Aldric the merchant was about to buy ten bottles.

Bayes the sparrow landed on the fox’s sign. “Half of what?” she asked.

The fox’s whiskers twitched. “Er, half the risk, of course.”

So Bayes flew to the village infirmary, where the badger healer kept careful records. The healer laid out a thousand pebbles on the floor. “Out of every thousand creatures in the marsh, about two catch marsh fever each year,” she said, placing two red pebbles among the grey ones.

Then she took one red pebble away. “With the fox’s tonic, it’s about one in a thousand.”

Aldric squinted at the floor. “So the tonic does halve the risk. From two pebbles to one.”

“Yes,” said the healer, “but look at the whole floor. Your chance drops from two in a thousand to one in a thousand. That’s a difference of just one creature in every thousand. A thousand creatures would need to take the tonic for one of them to be spared.”

Aldric looked at the price tag, then at the sea of grey pebbles. He put away nine of his coins.

Back at the market, the fox’s sign had been changed by a small, determined sparrow. It now read: “Two in a thousand down to one in a thousand.”

When someone says “half the risk”, always ask: half of what?

Relative risk compares the risk between two groups as a ratio, while absolute risk tells you the actual chance of something happening. A treatment that lowers risk from 2 in 1,000 to 1 in 1,000 gives a 50% relative risk reduction, which sounds huge, but the absolute risk reduction is only 0.1 percentage points, or 1 in 1,000. Another useful number is the “number needed to treat”: here, about 1,000 people would need the treatment for one person to benefit. Health headlines and advertisements often quote relative risk because it sounds more impressive, so understanding both numbers is an important part of health literacy and informed decision-making.

A moment to reflect

The next time a result is described as “half the risk”, what else will you ask to see?

If a thousand creatures must take the tonic for one to be spared, how does that change what you would spend?
