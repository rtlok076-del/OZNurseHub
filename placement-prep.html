Tool 1 / Brain sheet

Plan your shift on one page.

Fill it in on screen after handover, or print a blank one to write on. It prints on one landscape A4 page.

Protect patient privacy. Use bed numbers, and initials at most. Never enter names, dates of birth, record numbers or Medicare numbers. If you print it, follow your facility's rules for handover sheets, including disposing of them securely.

    Tool 4 / What can I do?

    What can I do in my year? Check, don't guess.

    There's no single list for every student. What you can do depends on what your uni has taught and assessed, your facility's policies and your supervising RN on the day.

    My year level

    Ask on day one

    • What has my uni taught and assessed me on so far? Bring your skills record if you have one.
    • Which activities does this facility let students do, and which need direct supervision?
    • Who's my supervising RN each shift, and who do I go to if they're busy?
    • How do I say so if I'm asked to do something outside my scope?

    Check, don't assume

    Rules for students often differ in these areas. Ask before you're involved.

    • Medicines that need extra checks or are legally restricted, such as Schedule 8 medicines
    • Intravenous medicines, fluids and blood products
    • Acting as a second checker
    • Taking verbal or phone orders
    • Electronic records and logins
    • Invasive devices and higher-risk procedures

    My scope tracker

    Tick as you confirm each one. Saved on this device. Add skills from your uni's list. To record each time you practise a skill, use the clinical skills logbook.

    Tool 5 / Feedback reflection

    Turn feedback into your next step.

    Many Australian nursing courses assess placements with the ANSAT (Australian Nursing Standards Assessment Tool), which is mapped to the NMBA Registered nurse standards for practice. Use this before your mid-placement and final assessments, or after any feedback.

    By standard Open the ones that apply

    Self-ratings use the ANSAT's 1 to 5 scale and are for your own reflection. Your assessor rates you against what's expected at your year level, and on the ANSAT a 1 or 2 means that standard hasn't been achieved. Your uni's form and instructions come first.

    Reflect: what, so what, now what?

    Summary for my assessment

    Keep it de-identified. Don't include patient or staff names or details that could identify anyone.
