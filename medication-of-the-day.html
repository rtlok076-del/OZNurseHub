Add it to your phone

One tap from your home screen.

Add this page to your home screen and it opens like an app, straight to today's card.

You're already using the home-screen version. Nice! There's nothing more to set up.

What you'll get: a Med of Day icon on your home screen. Tap it and today's card opens full-screen in its own window, without the browser's address bar or the rest of this page. It's built to feel like a widget. Preview the card view

What it isn't: it's a home-screen shortcut to a web app, not a native home-screen widget or an app from the App Store or Google Play. It won't show the medicine on your home screen until you tap it, and it won't send reminders.

iPhone

Using Safari

  1. Open this page in Safari.
  2. Tap the More button (•••), then tap Share.On some Safari layouts and older versions of iOS, the Share button (a square with an arrow pointing up) is on the toolbar. Tap that instead.
  3. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen.Can't see it? Scroll to the bottom, tap Edit Actions, then add Add to Home Screen.
  4. Check Open as Web App is turned on, so it opens straight into the card view.You'll see this option on iOS 26 and later. If it's off, the icon opens this full page in Safari instead.
  5. Tap Add. Look for the Med of Day icon on your Home Screen.

Steps based on Apple's guide, Turn a website into an app in Safari on iPhone.

Android

Using Chrome

  1. Open this page in Chrome.
  2. Tap More (⋮) to the right of the address bar.
  3. Tap Add to home screen.In some versions of Chrome, this is called Install and create shortcut.
  4. Tap Install so it opens straight into the card view.If you choose Create shortcut instead, the icon opens this full page in a normal Chrome tab.
  5. Follow the on-screen steps to finish. Look for the Med of Day icon on your home screen or in your apps.

Steps based on Google's guides, Use web apps and Create shortcuts for websites.

Menus vary a little between phones and versions. Using another browser? Look for Add to Home screen or Install in its menu. To remove the icon later, touch and hold it, then choose the option to remove or uninstall it.

Questions

Good to know.

Does it cost anything?

No. Medication of the Day is free, like everything on OzNurseHub, and you don't need an account. Adding it to your home screen is free too. Nothing is downloaded from an app store.

Does it work offline?

Yes, once you've opened it with an internet connection. Your phone keeps a copy of this page and the medicine list. Today's medicine is worked out on your phone from the date, so it still changes each day when you're offline.

If you've added it to an iPhone Home Screen, open it from the icon once while you're online so that version saves its own copy. Offline, the fonts may look a little different. Other OzNurseHub pages will only work if your phone has already saved them.

How much data does it use?

The page itself is small: about 140 KB, including the 100-medicine list, styles and fonts. The first time you visit OzNurseHub, your browser also saves the site's main pages and images for offline use, which adds about 4 to 5 MB in the background. After that, your phone reuses its saved copy and only checks for updates, so daily visits use very little data. Opening it on Wi-Fi the first time is a good idea if your data is limited.

Does it collect any information about me?

This page doesn't need an account, doesn't use cookies or analytics, and the card doesn't save your answers. Your browser keeps an offline copy of the site's files on your phone. The page's fonts come from Google Fonts, so your browser makes a standard request to Google when it loads them.

Is it a real home-screen widget?

Not quite. Apple and Google widgets have to come from an app installed from their app stores, and OzNurseHub is a website. What you get is the closest thing a website can offer: an icon that opens straight into a full-screen card, without the browser around it. It doesn't show the medicine on your home screen until you tap the icon.

Will it remind me to study?

No. It doesn't send notifications. Making it part of a routine helps, for example opening it on the bus or before a shift.

Why does everyone get the same medicine? When does it change?

Everyone sees the same medicine each day, so you can quiz a friend or your study group. It changes at midnight Adelaide time (Australia/Adelaide). Over time it works through all 100 medicines. Next one → gives you extra cards, and today's card comes back the next time you open the page.

Does it tell me doses?

No. The cards cover drug classes, selected uses and key risks for study only. Always use current local policies, medicine information and supervision in practice.

Where does the information come from?

The cards use the same 100 medicines as the Medication Library and the Learning Lab. You can read about their sources in Australian references & review scope.

It's not working. What can I try?
  • If it says “Loading today's medicine…”, check JavaScript is turned on in your browser, then refresh.
  • If it says “Medicine cards are resting today”, the medicine list didn't load. Check your connection and try again.
  • If you still see an older version after an update, close the page or app fully, then open it again.
