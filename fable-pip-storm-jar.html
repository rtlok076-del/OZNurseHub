Fable Story

Pip and the Storm Jar

A small story about a wombat who learns that a simple jar she can carry beats trying to catch the whole storm.

Watch · 1 minuteRead · 1 minuteLearning & reflection

The full story

A simple jar you can carry beats the whole storm.

High in a monastery of clay jars, a young wombat named Pip tried to catch a storm. But every time, the jar was far too small.

The old abbot smiled. “The whole truth will never fit. So don’t force it. Pick a simpler shape you can hold, then tune it to match.”

So Pip made a stand-in jar, with just a few easy swirls. Each night, she compared it to the storm, and nudged it a little closer.

Not perfect. But close, and light enough to carry.

That’s variational inference. When the exact answer is too big to compute, pick a simple family, and tune it until it fits best.

Wisdom isn’t holding the whole storm. It’s the best model you can carry, and knowing how close it is.

A moment to reflect

Where are you still trying to hold the whole storm, when a simpler model would get you moving?

Once you pick a stand-in, how do you check how close it is — and when do you stop nudging?
