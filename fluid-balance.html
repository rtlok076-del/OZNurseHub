Tool / Fluid balance chart

Pick a patient. Total the chart.

Each patient has a full 24-hour chart, with hourly rows from 08:00. In practice mode you type the running totals straight into the chart and check them. Switch to "Show all totals" to see the worked chart.

Method

How to total a fluid balance chart.

  1. Add down each intake column (oral, IV, NG or enteral, other) for the 24 hours. Then add the column totals together to get total intake.
  2. Do the same for each output column (urine, vomit, drain, NG aspirate, stool or other) to get total output.
  3. Balance = total intake − total output. More in than out is a positive balance (+). More out than in is a negative balance (−).
  4. Check your sums twice, or add across the rows as well as down the columns. The two methods should give the same answer.
  5. Look at the trend, not just the total. A balance can look fine at 07:00 while one column has been falling all afternoon.

Teaching notes

Common pitfalls.

Unmeasured lossesVomit on the floor, an incontinent episode or a leaking drain still count. Write "not measured" with a description (for example "large" or "pad soaked") and tell the RN. Never make up a number.
Insensible lossesBreathing, sweating and fever all lose fluid that can't be measured. Note them (for example "febrile, sweaty overnight") but don't chart a guessed volume.
Totalling errorsMost mistakes are simple maths: a missed row, a carried digit or a wrong sign on the balance. Total down and across, and get a second check when you're unsure.
Time and type of fluidRecord the time and what it was (water, tea, soup, jelly, IV bag, flush). Jelly, ice chips, soup and IV flushes are intake too, which matters most on a fluid restriction.
Chart as you goWrite each volume when it happens, not from memory at the end of the shift. Empty and measure drains and catheter bags at the times your ward uses.
Trends need actionA sustained drop in urine output, or output below the threshold in your local policy, needs escalating. So does a balance heading the wrong way on a fluid restriction. Report to the team leader or RN and follow your local policy, using ISBAR.
